Survey Findings

Future of Flight Equity surveyed 25 Wisconsin schools to examine access to aviation education, interest in future programming, and the support schools need to make those opportunities possible.

At a glance

The opportunity exists. Access does not.

Survey responses reveal a large gap between the number of schools currently offering aviation education and the number open to expanding it with adequate support.

Program availability

Most students lack access through their schools.

Only 12% of responding schools reported currently offering an aviation or aerospace course or program. Another 8% indicated that a program was in development.

The remaining 80% reported no current aviation or aerospace offering, demonstrating that formal access remains uncommon across the responding schools.

For many Wisconsin students, discovering aviation still depends on opportunities outside the regular school environment.
Support schools need

Funding and qualified instructors lead the list.

Respondents could select two forms of support that would be most helpful. Funding and finding a qualified instructor were each selected by 19 of the 25 respondents.

Funding 19 responses · 76%
Finding a qualified instructor 19 responses · 76%
Ready-to-teach curriculum 9 responses · 36%
Equipment 6 responses · 24%
Teacher professional development 3 responses · 12%
Interest in expansion

Schools are open to new aviation opportunities.

Respondents were asked whether their schools would be interested in adding aviation or aerospace courses if support were available.

60%

Selected “Maybe”

A majority expressed potential interest, suggesting that practical support and clear implementation options could help schools move from consideration to action.

32%

Selected “Yes”

Nearly one-third directly indicated interest in adding aviation or aerospace courses if support were available.
