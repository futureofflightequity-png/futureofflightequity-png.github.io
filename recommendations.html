Practical pathways forward

Recommendations

Expanding aviation education does not always require a new course, expensive equipment, or a complete curriculum overhaul. Schools can begin with flexible, affordable steps that build interest and capacity over time.

Our approach

Lower the barriers to getting started.

Survey findings show that funding, qualified instructors, curriculum, equipment, and professional development are major obstacles for schools.

The most realistic path is to use resources schools already have, introduce aviation gradually, and build partnerships that reduce cost and staff burden.

Start small. Learn what works. Expand over time.

Short modules, existing STEM courses, guest speakers, clubs, and community partnerships can give students meaningful aviation exposure without requiring a large initial investment.

Five recommendations

Realistic strategies for Wisconsin schools.

These recommendations are designed to be affordable, flexible, and scalable across schools with different budgets, staffing, and community resources.

1

Integrate aviation into existing STEM classes

Schools can use aviation examples in physics, mathematics, engineering, and technology lessons. Topics such as motion, energy, navigation, and system design already connect naturally with existing coursework.

2

Test short introductory modules

One- or two-week units can introduce aviation careers and concepts through digital resources, simulations, and hands-on problem solving without creating a full course.

3

Build local aviation partnerships

Flight schools, airports, museums, universities, aviation organizations, and industry professionals can provide guest speakers, mentors, facility visits, and real-world learning.

4

Begin with extracurricular opportunities

Clubs, seminars, workshops, and after-school activities require fewer approvals and allow schools to test student interest before making larger commitments.

5

Share resources across schools

A shared collection of lesson plans, funding opportunities, partner contacts, and implementation guides can reduce repeated work and make aviation education easier to launch.

+

Expand based on results

Schools can use participation, student feedback, and community support to decide whether to expand into longer modules, electives, certifications, or career pathways.

A low-risk starting model

A school can begin in three steps.

The goal is not to build a complete aviation program immediately. It is to create a practical first opportunity for students.

Step one

Identify an entry point

Choose one existing class, club, enrichment period, or event where aviation content can be introduced.

Step two

Add outside support

Invite a local aviation professional, use a ready-made lesson, or connect with a nearby organization or training program.

Step three

Measure interest

Gather student participation and feedback, then use the results to decide how the opportunity could grow.

Shared responsibility

Expanding access requires collaboration.

Schools cannot overcome every financial and structural obstacle alone. Aviation organizations, community partners, and education leaders all have a role.

🏫

Schools and educators

Test affordable modules, connect aviation to current classes, create clubs, and communicate what support is needed.

✈️

Aviation organizations

Offer guest lessons, mentorship, curriculum support, facility access, career exposure, and community partnerships.

🏛️

Leaders and policymakers

Raise awareness, support resource sharing, and create targeted funding opportunities that can grow over time.
